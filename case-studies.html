01.

Conversions API

CRM → META · SERVER-SIDE SIGNAL

Teaching Meta what a good lead looks like

n8n (self-hosted)Meta Conversions APILead AdsCRM webhooksSHA-256 hashing

// THE PROBLEM

An international education consultancy ran Lead Ads in many countries. Meta optimised for form fills, so it found people who filled forms, not people who became students. An audit of the CRM → Meta dataset showed that only "Closed Won" events were firing. That is too rare and too slow for Meta's algorithm to learn from.

// WHAT I BUILT

  • A self-hosted n8n workflow that receives each Lead Ads lead, pulls the full lead from the Graph API and creates it in the CRM.
  • A second workflow that listens for CRM stage changes and sends Lead → Qualified Lead → Converted back to Meta as server-side events.
  • Hashed email and phone (E.164, SHA-256), the lead_id kept for matching, and event_id used for deduplication.
  • gclid / fbclid / ctwa_clid capture so Google and click-to-WhatsApp leads can be attributed too.

// FLOW

Lead Ad form→n8n webhook→Graph API: fetch lead→CRM: create lead→Sales updates stage→n8n: map + hash→Meta CAPI event
BEFORE

1 event type (Closed Won), so Meta had almost no signal. The algorithm optimised for whoever filled forms fastest.

AFTER

A full funnel of events reaches Meta within minutes of each stage change. Campaigns could be optimised toward qualified leads, and the account moved from chasing lead volume to lead quality. Lead conversion rate rose 3% in 6 weeks alongside funnel and creative work.

02.

WhatsApp CRM

CHROME EXTENSION · ZOHO · N8N

Getting WhatsApp sales chats into the CRM

Chrome ExtensionWhatsApp WebZoho CRMn8n webhooksAI replies

// THE PROBLEM

Most of the selling happened on WhatsApp, but none of it reached the CRM. Lead stages were updated by hand, if at all. Follow-ups lived in people's heads, and reporting on the sales pipeline was guesswork. Paid WhatsApp CRM platforms cost a recurring fee per user and would have meant changing how the team works.

// WHAT I BUILT

  • A Chrome extension that turns WhatsApp Web into a sales workspace: a lead sidebar next to every chat and a Kanban pipeline.
  • Reply templates, scheduled follow-ups, keyword-triggered chatbot flows and AI-suggested replies.
  • Every new lead and stage change goes out as a webhook to n8n, which writes it into Zoho.
  • Built in-house with AI tools instead of buying a SaaS subscription.

// FLOW

WhatsApp chat→Extension sidebar→Stage change→Webhook→n8n→Zoho CRM→CAPI (Case 01)
BEFORE

WhatsApp conversations were invisible to the CRM. Stages were stale, follow-ups were missed, and click-to-WhatsApp ads couldn't be judged on the leads they produced.

AFTER

The sales team works from one screen and the CRM updates itself. WhatsApp stages now feed the Conversions API pipeline, so click-to-WhatsApp campaigns get quality signals too. No per-user SaaS fee.

03.

Geo budget control

META ADS · ACCOUNT STRUCTURE

Stopping big markets from eating the budget

Meta AdsABO vs CBOGeo-targetingAudience fatigueAds Library research

// THE PROBLEM

Campaigns targeted 22+ countries in grouped ad sets. Meta pushed spend toward the cheapest, most populous markets, so the small high-value markets that mattered to the business barely got delivery. Meanwhile, tiny audiences in island markets kept seeing the same ads until they tuned them out.

// WHAT I CHANGED

  • Restructured into per-country ABO ad sets grouped by region (GCC, Asia, Europe, Africa, Oceania), so every market got a guaranteed budget.
  • Added fatigue rules for small markets (e.g. Mauritius, Maldives, Seychelles): frequency watch, creative rotation, cooldowns.
  • Used Meta Ads Library competitor research to rewrite underperforming ads.
  • For a new domestic product, launched campaigns with sharp messaging aimed at working professionals.

BEFORE

Spend followed population, not priority. Small markets were starved or burned out.

AFTER

Budget follows business priority, and every market is controlled and measured on its own. Fatigue in small markets is managed instead of ignored.
